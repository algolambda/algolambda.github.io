Fractional CTO · AI Product Architect

Senior technical judgment. Hands-on when it matters.

I'm Giulio De Luise. I help founders make technical decisions that become expensive when they're wrong — what to build, what to automate, how to architect it, and what needs to be true before it can scale.

I work a few days a week with early-stage companies that need CTO-level ownership before they need a full-time CTO.

01 — What I do

Difficult technical decisions, owned end to end.

/01

Technical direction

I turn product ambiguity into clear technical decisions: architecture, build-vs-buy, AI strategy, roadmap and investment priorities.

The goal isn't the most sophisticated technology. It's the right technology for the stage you're at.

  • Architecture that survives the next stage without designing for the next decade
  • Build-vs-buy decisions grounded in economics and constraints
  • Technical strategy founders, engineers and investors can understand
  • Independent judgment when several directions look plausible
/02

AI-native products & systems

AI has made implementation faster. It has also made it easier to build systems nobody fully understands.

I design AI products around reliability, evidence, cost and control — not demos.

That includes LLM applications, agentic workflows, structured outputs, evaluations, observability, inference economics and the boundaries between autonomous and human decisions.

I stay close to implementation when needed, because architecture without contact with the real system quickly becomes theory.

  • Production AI systems, not prototype-only integrations
  • Explicit autonomy and human-approval boundaries
  • Evaluation, observability and cost designed in from the start
  • Agents operating under engineering constraints, not unlimited permission
/03

Engineering leverage

The question is no longer simply how many engineers you need.

It's how effectively people, agents and systems can work together without quality becoming accidental.

I help build that environment: specifications, architecture constraints, delivery practices, verification, CI, code review and hiring.

  • Agent-assisted engineering with evidence and control
  • Standards enforced by systems, not memory
  • Senior technical support for the existing team
  • Hiring when people genuinely become the constraint

02 — About

I've spent 25 years seeing what happens after the original technical decision.

I started in enterprise software, then built products, platforms and data systems for high-growth technology companies, led engineering and data teams, founded companies, and worked through the consequences of systems meeting real users, scale and commercial pressure.

Today my work sits at the intersection of technology leadership, product judgment and AI-native engineering.

I still build.

Not because clients need another pair of hands, but because staying close to production leads to better decisions.

How an engagement runs

  1. 01

    Understand

    We start with the business situation, not the stack.

    What decision is difficult? What is becoming a constraint? What happens if nothing changes?

  2. 02

    Decide

    I inspect enough of the product, architecture, team and data to separate symptoms from causes.

    Then we make the important choices explicit: keep, change, stop, build, buy, automate or defer.

  3. 03

    Intervene

    I work alongside the team where implementation is necessary to validate or carry through those decisions.

    The objective is progress, not utilisation.

  4. 04

    Leave leverage behind

    The company should need less of me over time.

    That means better systems, clearer architecture, stronger engineers, better automation and decisions that don't need to be rediscovered six months later.

03 — When to call me

Usually, something important is uncertain.

Algo Lambda is useful when:

  1. 01

    your product works, but you're not confident the architecture will survive the next stage;

  2. 02

    your AI prototype works in a demo but behaves unpredictably in production;

  3. 03

    coding agents have increased output, but nobody knows whether they have improved the engineering system;

  4. 04

    several technical directions look reasonable, and choosing one commits months of effort;

  5. 05

    you already have engineers but need senior decisions across product, technology and business;

  6. 06

    you need CTO-level ownership now, but a permanent CTO would be premature;

  7. 07

    or an investor, customer or acquisition makes it important to know what your technology can really support.

04 — Questions

What founders ask before we start

Do you actually write code?

Yes, when code is the fastest way to understand, validate or unblock the problem.

But you're not hiring me for coding capacity. You're hiring me to take responsibility for difficult technical decisions and help make them real.

How much of your time do I get?

Usually one to three days a week.

The right level depends on the problem and stage of the company. I prefer enough involvement to own outcomes rather than attend meetings.

We already have engineers. Does this still work?

Often that's the best situation.

I can focus on architecture, product and technical trade-offs, AI strategy, engineering systems and the decisions that would otherwise consume the founder or senior team.

What does it cost?

A monthly retainer or bounded engagement, agreed before we start.

The model depends on whether you need ongoing technical ownership or a specific intervention.

Cash or equity?

Primarily cash. For the right company and relationship, a combination can make sense.

Are you replacing our future CTO?

Usually not.

A successful engagement often makes it clearer what kind of CTO the company should eventually hire.

What happens when you leave?

The important decisions are documented, systems are operable, ownership sits with the team and there is no dependency on Algo Lambda.

No lock-in.

Remote or on-site?

Remote-first from London, with on-site work when it materially improves the outcome.
